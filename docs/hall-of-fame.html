Nuggets Hall of Fame

13 members of the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame have Denver ties: 11 as players and 2 as coaches or contributors. Players with short Denver stints are flagged. After them come 25 franchise legends not in the Hall, including the five starters from the 2023 championship team, then two icons from behind the bench and in the stands.

As of Thu Oct 1, 2026 · Includes the Class of 2026 (enshrined Aug 2026)

Naismith Hall of Famers

Players

Alex English

Forward · Nuggets 1980–1990

HOF 1997 · Player

  • Eight straight All-Star selections with Denver (1982–89) and the 1982–83 NBA scoring title (28.4 ppg).
  • Averaged 25.9 points over 837 games in 11 Nuggets seasons; still the franchise’s career scoring leader, and scored more points than any NBA player during the 1980s.
  • No. 2 retired by the Nuggets in 1993.

Dan Issel

Center/forward; later head coach and executive · Nuggets Player 1975–1985; head coach 1992–95 and 1999–2001

HOF 1993 · Player

  • Averaged 20.7 points and 8.3 rebounds over 802 games in 10 Denver seasons (ABA and NBA); All-Star in 1976 (ABA) and 1977 (NBA).
  • No. 44 retired by the Nuggets in 1985.
  • Coached Denver for parts of six seasons (180–208), including the 1993–94 team that upset top-seeded Seattle in the first round.

David Thompson

Guard/forward · Nuggets 1975–1982

HOF 1996 · Player

  • 1975–76 ABA Rookie of the Year (26.0 ppg); All-NBA First Team in 1976–77 and 1977–78; All-Star Game MVP in 1976 (ABA) and 1979 (NBA).
  • Scored 73 points in the 1977–78 regular-season finale at Detroit (April 9, 1978).
  • Averaged 24.1 points over 498 games with Denver; No. 33 retired in 1992.

Dikembe Mutombo

Center · Nuggets 1991–1996

HOF 2015 · Player

  • 1994–95 NBA Defensive Player of the Year, the first of his four DPOY awards; All-Star with Denver in 1992, 1995 and 1996.
  • Anchored the 1994 Nuggets, the first No. 8 seed to beat a No. 1 seed (Seattle) in NBA playoff history.
  • Averaged 12.3 rebounds and 3.8 blocks over 391 Denver games; No. 55 retired in 2016.

Carmelo Anthony

Forward · Nuggets 2003–2011

HOF 2025 · Player

  • No. 3 pick in 2003; averaged 21.0 ppg as a rookie as Denver went from 17–65 to 43–39 and a playoff berth.
  • Averaged 24.8 points over 564 Nuggets games; All-NBA Third Team 2006, 2007 and 2009, Second Team 2010; All-Star 2007, 2008 and 2010 as a Nugget.
  • Led Denver to the playoffs in all seven of his full seasons, including the 2009 Western Conference finals.

Bobby Jones

Forward · Nuggets 1974–1978

HOF 2019 · Player

  • All-Defensive First Team in all four Denver seasons (ABA 1975, 1976; NBA 1977, 1978).
  • All-Star in 1976 (ABA), 1977 and 1978 (NBA); runner-up for 1975–76 ABA MVP.
  • Averaged 14.8 points, 8.6 rebounds and 2.0 steals over 324 games with Denver.

Spencer Haywood

Forward/center (Denver Rockets, ABA) · Nuggets 1969–70

HOF 2015 · PlayerOne season in Denver

  • 1969–70 ABA MVP, Rookie of the Year and All-Star Game MVP, the franchise’s first league MVP.
  • Averaged 30.0 points and 19.5 rebounds, leading the ABA in both.
  • Named to the ABA All-Time Team.

Chauncey Billups

Guard · Nuggets 1998–2000; 2008–2011

HOF 2024 · Player

  • Denver native (George Washington High School, University of Colorado).
  • Acquired from Detroit in the November 2008 Allen Iverson trade; led the 2008–09 Nuggets to 54 wins and the Western Conference finals.
  • 2010 All-Star; averaged 16.9 points and 5.3 assists over 259 Denver games.

Walter Davis

Guard/forward · Nuggets 1988–1991; 1991–92

HOF 2024 · PlayerLate-career Nuggets stint

  • Averaged 15.6 points over 235 games in four Denver seasons, including 17.5 ppg in 1989–90 and 18.7 ppg in 1990–91.
  • Six-time All-Star and 1977–78 Rookie of the Year with Phoenix before joining Denver.
  • Inducted posthumously in 2024.

George McGinnis

Forward · Nuggets 1978–1980

HOF 2017 · PlayerBrief Nuggets stint (two seasons)

  • 1979 All-Star with Denver (22.6 points, 11.4 rebounds in 1978–79).
  • Came from Philadelphia in the 1978 trade for Bobby Jones; traded to Indiana in February 1980 for Alex English.
  • 1974–75 ABA co-MVP and two-time ABA champion with Indiana.

Allen Iverson

Guard · Nuggets 2006–2008

HOF 2016 · PlayerBrief Nuggets stint (about two seasons)

  • 2008 All-Star with Denver; averaged 26.4 points over all 82 games in 2007–08.
  • Averaged 25.6 points and 7.1 assists over 135 Nuggets games.
  • Traded to Detroit in November 2008 for Chauncey Billups.

Coaches and contributors

Larry Brown

Head coach; earlier a Rockets guard · Nuggets Coach 1974–1979; player 1970–72

HOF 2002 · Coach

  • ABA Coach of the Year in 1974–75 and 1975–76 with Denver; the 1974–75 team went 65–19, the best record in franchise history.
  • Went 251–134 in Denver, finishing first four straight seasons (1974–75 to 1977–78) and reaching the 1976 ABA Finals.
  • Played two seasons at point guard for the Denver Rockets (1970–72), averaging 6.9 assists.

George Karl

Head coach · Nuggets 2005–2013

HOF 2022 · Coach

  • Went 423–257 and made the playoffs in all nine seasons he coached (2004–05 to 2012–13).
  • Led the 2008–09 team to the Western Conference finals.
  • 2012–13 NBA Coach of the Year after a 57–25 season, then the franchise’s best NBA record (since matched in 2023–24).

Induction years and categories: hoophall.com, Basketball-Reference, NBA.com

Nuggets legends (not in HOF)

Retired numbers: 2 English, 12 Lever, 33 Thompson, 40 Beck, 44 Issel, 55 Mutombo, plus the “432” banner for Doug Moe. Bill Russell’s No. 6 is retired league-wide.

Lafayette “Fat” Lever

Guard · Nuggets 1984–1990

  • Averaged 17.0 points, 7.6 rebounds, 7.5 assists and 2.5 steals over 474 Denver games; All-Star in 1988 and 1990, All-NBA Second Team in 1986–87.
  • Denver made the playoffs in all six of his seasons, including the 1985 Western Conference finals.
  • No. 12 retired on December 2, 2017.

Byron Beck

Forward/center · Nuggets 1967–1977

  • Original 1967 Denver Rocket who spent his whole 10-year career with the franchise, nine seasons in the ABA and one in the NBA.
  • ABA All-Star in 1969 and 1976.
  • No. 40 retired in 1977, the first number the franchise retired.

Doug Moe

Head coach (assistant 1974–76) · Nuggets 1980–1990

  • Won 432 regular-season games, a franchise record until Michael Malone passed it in 2024–25; honored with the “432” banner in 2002.
  • 1987–88 NBA Coach of the Year (54–28); made the playoffs in nine straight seasons (1982–1990), including the 1985 West finals.
  • Died February 17, 2026.

Michael Malone

Head coach · Nuggets 2015–2025

  • Coached the 2023 NBA champions, who went 16–4 in the playoffs.
  • Won 471 regular-season games, the most by any coach in franchise history.
  • Made the playoffs six straight seasons (2019–2024), including the 2020 and 2023 West finals.

Kiki Vandeweghe

Forward; later general manager · Nuggets Player 1980–1984; GM 2001–2006

  • All-Star in 1983 and 1984; averaged 29.4 ppg in 1983–84 and 23.3 ppg over 293 Denver games.
  • Played in the 186–184 triple-overtime loss to Detroit on December 13, 1983, still the highest-scoring game in NBA history.
  • Returned as Denver’s general manager from 2001 to 2006.

T.R. Dunn

Guard · Nuggets 1980–1988; 1989–90

  • Acquired from Portland in August 1980; All-Defensive Second Team three straight seasons (1982–83, 1983–84, 1984–85).
  • Received Defensive Player of the Year votes in 1983–84 and 1984–85 (seventh both years) and 1986–87 (eighth); had 173 steals (2.2 per game) in 1983–84.
  • Played 734 games over parts of 10 Denver seasons (4.9 points, 4.8 rebounds, 1.5 steals), including all 82 in five different seasons.

Bill Hanzlik

Forward · Nuggets 1982–1990

  • Acquired from Seattle in June 1982 in the trade that sent David Thompson to the SuperSonics.
  • All-Defensive Second Team in 1985–86, when he averaged 12.5 points in 79 games, all off the bench; career-high 13.0 points per game in 1986–87.
  • Averaged 7.7 points and 3.0 assists over 593 games in eight Denver seasons.

Danny Schayes

Center · Nuggets 1983–1990

  • Acquired from Utah in February 1983 for Rich Kelley; traded to Milwaukee in August 1990 for Terry Mills.
  • Best season in 1987–88: 13.9 points and 8.2 rebounds in 81 games (74 starts), shooting .540 from the field (11th in the NBA) and making 407 free throws.
  • Averaged 9.4 points and 5.8 rebounds over 536 Denver games, shooting .809 from the line.

Calvin Natt

Forward · Nuggets 1984–1989

  • Came from Portland with Fat Lever, Wayne Cooper and two draft picks in the June 1984 trade for Kiki Vandeweghe.
  • 1985 All-Star with Denver; averaged 23.3 points (11th in the NBA) on .546 shooting and 7.8 rebounds in 1984–85.
  • Averaged 17.8 points over 189 games in parts of five Denver seasons before a January 1989 trade to San Antonio.

Blair Rasmussen

Center · Nuggets 1985–1991

  • Drafted 15th overall in 1985 with the Portland pick Denver received in the Kiki Vandeweghe trade.
  • Averaged 12.7 points in 1987–88; in 1990–91 he started 69 games and averaged 12.5 points, 9.7 rebounds (11th in the NBA) and 1.9 blocks.
  • Averaged 10.1 points and 6.0 rebounds over 429 games in six Denver seasons.

Michael Adams

Point guard · Nuggets 1987–1991

  • Made at least one 3-pointer in 79 straight games (January 28, 1988, to January 23, 1989), then an NBA record.
  • Averaged 26.5 points (sixth in the NBA) and 10.5 assists (third) in 1990–91, including 54 points against Milwaukee on March 23, 1991.
  • Averaged 18.2 points and 7.2 assists over 304 Denver games and made 630 3-pointers; acquired from Washington in November 1987 and traded back to the Bullets in June 1991.

Marcus Camby

Center · Nuggets 2002–2008

  • 2006–07 NBA Defensive Player of the Year with Denver.
  • All-Defensive First Team in 2007 and 2008, Second Team in 2005 and 2006.
  • Averaged 11.1 rebounds and 3.0 blocks over 372 Denver games.

Antonio McDyess

Forward · Nuggets 1995–1997; 1999–2002

  • Acquired on draft night in 1995 after the Clippers took him No. 2 overall; All-Rookie First Team in 1995–96 (13.4 points, 7.5 rebounds).
  • All-NBA Third Team in 1998–99 (21.2 points, 10.7 rebounds) after re-signing with Denver in January 1999; 2001 All-Star (20.8 points, 12.1 rebounds in 2000–01).
  • Averaged 18.2 points, 9.0 rebounds and 1.7 blocks over 361 Denver games; traded to New York in June 2002 in the deal that brought back Marcus Camby and Nenê.

Mahmoud Abdul-Rauf

Guard (formerly Chris Jackson) · Nuggets 1990–1996

  • No. 3 pick in 1990; All-Rookie Second Team in 1990–91 (14.1 points).
  • 1992–93 NBA Most Improved Player (19.2 points, up from 10.3); led the NBA in free-throw percentage in 1993–94 (.956) and 1995–96 (.930).
  • Started all 12 playoff games for the 1993–94 team that upset top-seeded Seattle; averaged 16.0 points over 439 Denver games.

LaPhonso Ellis

Forward · Nuggets 1992–1998

  • No. 5 pick in 1992; All-Rookie First Team in 1992–93 after starting all 82 games (14.7 points, 9.1 rebounds).
  • Led Denver in scoring (16.0 points, plus 8.0 rebounds) and started all five games in the 1994 first-round upset of top-seeded Seattle.
  • Averaged a career-high 21.9 points in 1996–97 and 15.2 points and 7.9 rebounds over 343 games in six Denver seasons.

Bryant Stith

Guard · Nuggets 1992–2000

  • No. 13 pick in 1992, using the New Jersey pick Denver got in the January 1991 three-team trade that sent Walter Davis to Portland.
  • Started all 82 games in 1993–94 (12.5 points), then started all five games of the first-round upset of top-seeded Seattle.
  • Averaged 10.9 points over 458 games in eight Denver seasons, shooting .840 from the line; traded to Boston in October 2000.

Rodney Rogers

Forward · Nuggets 1993–1995

  • Drafted by Denver No. 9 overall in 1993; averaged 8.1 points as a rookie and 9.0 off the bench in the five-game 1994 upset of top-seeded Seattle.
  • Averaged 12.2 points and 4.8 rebounds with 77 starts in 1994–95.
  • Traded with Brent Barry to the Los Angeles Clippers in June 1995 for Antonio McDyess and Randy Woods. Died November 21, 2025.

Nick Van Exel

Point guard · Nuggets 1998–2002

  • Acquired from the Los Angeles Lakers in June 1998 for Tony Battie and Tyronn Lue.
  • Averaged 9.0 assists (714 total) in 1999–2000 and 17.7 points in 2000–01; scored 21.4 per game in 45 games in 2001–02 before a February 2002 trade to Dallas.
  • Averaged 17.7 points and 8.4 assists over 245 Denver games, 243 of them starts.

Kenyon Martin

Forward · Nuggets 2004–2011

  • Acquired from New Jersey in July 2004 for three future first-round picks.
  • Averaged 12.3 points, 7.0 rebounds and 1.2 steals over 371 Denver games (361 starts) in seven seasons.
  • Started all 16 playoff games for the 2008–09 team that reached the Western Conference finals.

Nenê

Forward/center · Nuggets 2002–2012

  • Acquired on 2002 draft night (New York’s No. 7 pick) in the Antonio McDyess trade; All-Rookie First Team in 2002–03.
  • Led the NBA in field-goal percentage in 2010–11 (.615) after finishing second in 2008–09 (.604) and third in 2009–10 (.587).
  • Averaged 12.4 points and 7.0 rebounds over 555 games in parts of 10 seasons; started all 16 playoff games for the 2009 West finals team.

Nikola Jokić

Center · Nuggets 2015–present

🏆 2023 champion starter

  • Three-time NBA MVP (2020–21, 2021–22, 2023–24) and MVP runner-up in 2022–23, 2024–25 and 2025–26; drafted 41st overall in 2014.
  • Led Denver to its first NBA title in 2023 as Finals MVP and Western Conference finals MVP.
  • Averaged a triple-double in 2024–25 (29.6/12.7/10.2) and 2025–26 (27.7/12.9/10.7), and led the NBA in rebounds and assists in 2025–26; eight All-Star and eight All-NBA selections (six First Team).

Jamal Murray

Guard · Nuggets 2016–present

🏆 2023 champion starter

  • Starting point guard on the 2023 champions; No. 7 pick in 2016.
  • First All-Star and All-NBA (Third Team) selections in 2025–26, when he averaged a career-high 25.4 points.
  • Has spent his entire NBA career (nine seasons played) with Denver.

Kentavious Caldwell-Pope

Guard · Nuggets 2022–2024

🏆 2023 champion starter

  • Acquired from Washington in July 2022 with Ish Smith for Will Barton and Monte Morris.
  • Started all 20 playoff games for the 2023 champions; in 2022–23 he shot 42.3% from 3 (11th in the NBA) and ranked sixth with 112 steals.
  • Started all 152 games he played in two Denver seasons (10.4 points, 41.5% from 3) before signing with Orlando in July 2024.

Michael Porter Jr.

Forward · Nuggets 2018–2025

🏆 2023 champion starter

  • No. 14 pick in 2018; averaged 19.0 points on 44.5% 3-point shooting (ninth in the NBA) in 2020–21.
  • Started all 20 playoff games in the 2023 title run, averaging 13.4 points and 8.1 rebounds.
  • Averaged 16.2 points and 6.4 rebounds over 345 Denver games; traded to Brooklyn in July 2025 for Cameron Johnson.

Aaron Gordon

Forward · Nuggets 2021–present

🏆 2023 champion starter

  • Acquired from Orlando in March 2021 for Gary Harris, R.J. Hampton and a first-round pick; still on the roster entering 2026–27.
  • Started all 20 playoff games for the 2023 champions (13.3 points, 6.0 rebounds, 51.8% shooting).
  • Hit the winning 3-pointer with 3 seconds left in a 121–119 Game 1 win at Oklahoma City in the 2025 West semifinals; averaged 14.7 points over 328 Denver games through 2025–26.

🏆 2023 champion starter: started all five games of the 2023 NBA Finals vs. Miami (Murray, Caldwell-Pope, Porter Jr., Gordon, Jokić).

Behind the bench & in the stands

Not players or coaches, but part of the franchise’s story.

Bob “Chopper” Travaglini

Athletic trainer; later Nuggets Community Fund president · Nuggets 1976–1990

  • Denver’s trainer for its first 14 NBA seasons (1976–1990), after seven years as trainer of the ABA’s Washington Capitols and Virginia Squires; self-taught, with no formal medical training.
  • Started Chopper’s Dinner in 1987, a charity auction that became the biggest fund-raiser for the Nuggets and Avalanche, raising more than $200,000 a year for local children’s charities; later president of the Nuggets Community Fund.
  • Inducted into the Colorado Sports Hall of Fame in 2000, a year after his death at 66; Ball Arena’s address (1000 Chopper Circle) and the team’s Chopper Travaglini Community Award carry his name.

Rocky

Mascot (SuperMascot Rocky, a mountain lion) · Nuggets 1990–present

  • Debuted December 15, 1990, at a home game against Phoenix. Kenn Solomon, the original Rocky, performed the role until he retired in 2021, then returned to finish the 2022–23 championship season.
  • Inducted into the Mascot Hall of Fame (Class of 2008).
  • Famous for backward half-court shots; has performed for the NBA at All-Star Games and international events.
